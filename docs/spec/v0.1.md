## 1. Problem and product

A household cooks from a library of recipes scattered across websites, cookbooks and notes. Deciding what to cook each week, and then writing a shopping list from several recipes, is a weekly chore that existing recipe apps do not shorten, because they store recipes as text they do not understand.

Finders Eaters (working name) is a recipe book that **understands its recipes**. A household brings in the recipes it already cooks; the **engine** reads each into a structured form (every ingredient line becomes an amount, a unit, a known **ingredient** and its prep; every recipe gets **tags** across eight fixed **dimensions**). That makes the **library** searchable, and it lets the app help with the weekly decision: it suggests recipes that fit around the ones already chosen, **says why** for each, and turns the **plan** into a **shopping list** merged across recipes, sorted by **aisle**, that already knows the household has oil.

**V0.1 is the core loop for one household**: import, check, find, plan, shop. It is used by two adults on their phones in three moments: on the sofa planning, in the shop with poor signal, and just after finding a recipe online (design brief section 2).

## 2. Scope

### In V0.1

The cut from [Agree the V0.1 feature cut](https://github.com/Ed-Barnes937/FindersEaters/issues/2): every feature in `docs/ideation/features.md` scored 4 or 5 is in, score 3 is out, and ⚑ foundations shape the design without being built. Concretely:

- **Import**: paste a link (server fetch), share to the app (Android share target, iPhone Shortcut with an **import token**), and manual entry (paste-friendly form). Cookbook scanning (score 5) is pre-V1, out of V0.1.
- **Engine**: line parsing (kind, quantity, unit, ingredient, prep), **canonical measure** with conversions, **original text** kept and **re-parse**, auto-tagging, derived dietary tags, the **ingredient hierarchy** at seed depth, **optional line** marks.
- **Review and correct** a parsed recipe, with **corrections** as pins that survive re-parse.
- **Library**: Recipes screen (library and search are one screen), recipe view, favourites, **provenance** display, the **personal layer** (favourite in the UI; rating and notes stored structure only).
- **Search**: filter by tag, ingredient (with hierarchy) and time, compiled to the **query** model.
- **Scaling**: by servings, with **scaling notes**.
- **Planning**: plans as lists of **plan items** with optional dates; **suggestions** from **anchors** with overlap by **distinctiveness**, **variety**, favourites and effort; a **reason** on every suggestion; **skip** and **swap**.
- **Shopping**: computed list merging quantities, **ticks**, **always-haves** pre-ticked, crude aisle grouping, offline ticking.
- **Household**: default servings, units setting with one value (`uk_metric`), sign-in, sync across devices (server is the source of truth), two users sharing one household (created by CLI).

### Foundations (designed for, not built)

Household sharing UX, household members, sub-recipes, collections and **rules** (query plus min/max), dismiss with a reason, learned **cadence**, leftovers as plan items, meal grouping, semantic search, recipe images, free-text shopping extras. Each has a named, additive landing spot in `docs/spec/data-model.md` section 9 (one row amended by section 16 below).

### Out of V0.1

As listed in the map's Out of scope: household sharing UX and invites, cookbook page scanning and OCR, link packs and their curation, cookbook barcode import, the Capacitor wrapper and iOS share extension, household members, similar recipes, search beyond filters (natural language, semantic, saved collections, relational, "what can I make with"), planner extras (dismiss with reason, leftovers, presets, learned cadence, hard rules UI), active vs waiting time, round to buyable amounts, location selection, rating and notes UI, meal pairing, free-text shopping extras, "what we plan" setting (no one-meal-a-day assumption: meal types and count are suggestion parameters), season tags, public sign-up, password reset by email, passkeys, live shared-list push, recipe images, LLM calls of any kind.

## 3. User stories

Grouped by the moment of use. "We" is the household (two adults sharing one library, plan and list).

**Getting in**

1. As a household member, I sign in with email and password inside the installed app and stay signed in for months on that device.
2. As the admin (Ed), I create our household and both users with a CLI command, and reset a password the same way.

**Importing**

3. As a cook who just found a recipe on my phone, I share the page to the app (Android share sheet, or the "Add to Finders Eaters" Shortcut on iPhone) and it is in our library within seconds, even if the site blocks server fetches.
4. As a cook at a laptop or with a link, I paste a URL and the recipe is imported.
5. As a cook with a cookbook or our own recipe, I type or paste it into a plain form (title, book and page or "Our recipe", serves or makes, time, ingredients one per line, method), and the app structures it.
6. As a cook, I never lose what I imported: the recipe is saved before it is read, and if the reader is unavailable it shows "Still being read" and finishes later.
7. As a cook, when a page cannot be read I am offered manual entry with what could be captured.

**Checking**

8. As a cook, after import I see only what the app was unsure about, as questions in words with up to three one-tap answers, and the original line with the relevant words highlighted.
9. As a cook, I confirm all the routine lines in one tap ("Looks right - confirm N"), with undo.
10. As a cook, my corrections survive every later improvement to the reader.
11. As a cook, I can always see the original text of every line.
12. As a cook, a recipe is usable (searchable, plannable, on the list) before I have checked it; unknowns simply do not match filters.

**Finding**

13. As a household, I browse our whole library, newest first, or A to Z, quickest or longest since cooked.
14. As a household, I filter by meal, time, protein, carb, cuisine, diet, ingredient (where "chicken" includes chicken thighs), equipment, effort and our own tags, including "not" filters, and the URL keeps my search.
15. As a household, I see a dietary note wherever a diet tag appears, because derived diet tags are a guide, not a guarantee.
16. As a household, I open a recipe and see its provenance, facts, tags (each a link to a search), ingredients scaled to the servings I choose, method, favourite and Add to plan.

**Planning**

17. As a household on the sofa, I add a few recipes I want this week and ask for "3 more dinners", and get suggestions that share ingredients with what I chose, are not three pasta bakes, and each say why in plain words.
18. As a household, I tap Another to replace one suggestion, Swap to choose from the next five or search, Add or Add all to put them in the plan.
19. As a household, I put a plan item on a day or leave it undated, and set its servings.
20. As a household, when a plan is finished I start a new one, bringing over what we did not cook, without buying its ingredients twice.
21. As a household, I can look at and correct past plans (they are our cooking history).

**Shopping**

22. As a shopper with one hand on the trolley, I see one row per ingredient in our shop's walk order, merged across recipes with amounts, and tick a row with one tap (undo for 4 s); ticked rows move to In the basket.
23. As a shopper, always-haves (oil, salt) start ticked under At home, and I can untick one I have run out of.
24. As a shopper with no signal, the list keeps working and my ticks sync later.
25. As a shopper, if the plan grows after I ticked something, that item comes back unticked with "was N".
26. As a shopper, I can see which recipes each item is for.

**Settings**

27. As a household, I set default servings and edit our always-haves.
28. As an iPhone user, I add a device, get its import code once, install the Shortcut, and can see when each device last imported and revoke it.
29. As a household member, I can change my password, sign out, and sign out of other devices.

## 4. Architecture

Authoritative: `docs/adr/0001-stack-and-hosting.md`, `docs/spec/operations.md`.

- **Repo**: a standalone pnpm + Turborepo monorepo on HomeOfEd's stack (patterns copied, packages not shared). Node 24, TypeScript end to end, Zod, Vitest, Playwright.
- **Server**: Fastify with tRPC for the app's own API; plain versioned HTTP routes beside it for outside clients (`POST /api/v1/import`, `/health`). Better Auth mounted in Fastify.
- **Database**: Neon Postgres (London, pgvector available, not used in V0.1) through Drizzle. The food knowledge base lives in a `kb` schema.
- **Client**: React SPA with Vite, TanStack Router and Query, SCSS modules plus Base UI, installed as a PWA. Mutations persist and pause offline and replay on reconnect.
- **Hosting**: one Docker image per Fly app, scale-to-zero `shared-cpu-1x` 512 MB in `lhr`. Production `finders-eaters` from `main`, staging `finders-eaters-staging` from `develop`, each on its own Neon branch. About £1.75 a month at V0.1 load.
- **No job queue.** Engine work runs inside the request that needs it, with lazy completion on read and a one-off-machine batch re-parse.
- **Domain**: a `homeofed.com` subdomain for V0.1 (a product domain is a pre-V1 decision).

The deep modules below are pure (no I/O) unless noted, and each is the single owner of its rules. Package names are indicative; the module boundaries are the contract.

## 5. Data model

Authoritative: `docs/spec/data-model.md`, amended by section 16 (R1 to R3).

- **Durable vs derived.** Durable data (original text, provenance, corrections, personal layer, plans, plan items, ticks, always-haves, households, users) is relational and carefully migrated. Derived data (engine output, projections, needs-review, the KB) is rebuildable from durable data plus versions, so it changes shape by re-parse, never by data migration.
- **Ownership.** Every durable row belongs to a **household** directly or through one parent; every read is household-scoped in the data-access layer, with a test that asserts it. `household_user` joins users to households (unique on user in V0.1).
- **Recipe row**: identity, immutable `source` (the source document; a trigger rejects updates), provenance (`url` with site name, `book` with title and page text, or `own`; `image_url` captured, unused), engine run fields (`output` JSON, engine version, KB version, run status, ran at) and projected facts (title, title sort key, total minutes, yield kind, servings, unit system, needs review). Soft delete.
- **Engine output** is one JSON document per recipe (the effective output, corrections already applied as pins), plus rebuildable `recipe_tag` and `recipe_line` projections, all written atomically with compare-and-set on `revision`.
- **Corrections** are keyed by scope and anchor (line index, part index, field, line hash; or a recipe field or `tag:<dimension>:<value>`), carry a whole replacement value and an intent (`change`, `confirm`, `confirm_bulk`), and are never deleted by the engine. Orphaned is a per-run flag, not stored.
- **Personal layer**: `recipe_personal` (favourite; rating and notes with no UI), `user_tag`, `recipe_user_tag`.
- **Plans**: discrete `plan` rows with an optional date range; the **current plan** is the most recently created. `plan_item` is one recipe with servings (in the recipe's yield kind, null = as written), an optional date and a position. A dated plan item is a **cooking**; last cooked and times cooked are derived.
- **Shopping**: no stored list. One `shopping_tick` table (R1) plus `always_have`.
- **KB references**: real foreign keys from `recipe_line` and `always_have` into `kb.ingredient`, so a KB load upserts and retires, never deletes.
- **Conventions**: UUIDv7 ids, client-suppliable on create so replayed offline mutations are idempotent; `timestamptz` for instants and `date` for plan days; "today" in the household timezone (`Europe/London`); `created_by` on rows people create.

## 6. Auth and accounts

Authoritative: `docs/spec/auth.md`.

- Better Auth (Fastify plus Drizzle adapter, app-owned tables, UUIDv7 ids), **email and password only**. Magic links and OAuth break in the iOS Home Screen app's isolated storage; passkeys wait for the product domain.
- **No public sign-up.** A CLI command in the app image creates households and users and resets passwords. "Forgotten your password?" says to ask the admin.
- 90-day sliding, `SameSite=Lax` session cookie. Sign-in is rate-limited.
- Every tRPC request resolves session to user to household once, into a typed principal; handlers never read a household id from input.
- **Import tokens**: per device, `fe_imp_` plus a 256-bit secret, stored as a SHA-256 hash only, labelled, shown once, last-used visible, revoked immediately, no expiry, valid only while its creator is in the household. Sent as `Authorization: Bearer` to `POST /api/v1/import` only; that route ignores cookies and rate-limits 30 an hour per token.

## 7. Import capture

Authoritative: `docs/spec/import-capture.md`, from [Specify the import capture contract](https://github.com/Ed-Barnes937/FindersEaters/issues/24). Auth, 401/429 behaviour and the Bearer header stay in `docs/spec/auth.md` section 7.

- **Devices capture, the server extracts.** Every route hands one **import service** a **capture**: a link (URL, optionally with the page HTML the device already has) or a typed-in recipe. Nothing on a device parses recipes, so the iPhone Shortcut stays a dumb pipe and every extraction fix ships with the server. **The engine never fetches.**
- **Capture routes**: Paste a link and the Android share target (`/share`, first `http(s)` URL in `url`, `text`, then `title`) call the session-authenticated tRPC `recipe.import`, with a server fetch for HTML. The iPhone Shortcut posts `{ url, html?, client: "ios-shortcut/1" }` to `POST /api/v1/import` with its import token; from Safari it sends the rendered page, trimmed on the device (`docs/spec/ios-shortcut.md`), from another app only the URL. Type it in sends separate fields. Desktop has Paste a link and Type it in; the bookmarklet is later.
- **One pure extractor**, `extract(html, pageUrl)`, versioned and fixture-tested: JSON-LD first, then microdata, merged all-or-nothing with the page's HTML ingredient block to recover section headings and the commas JSON-LD drops. Method lines are `{ text, heading? }` like ingredient lines. Provenance keeps the site name and image URL.
- **Fetching**: a link with no device HTML gets one server fetch with an SSRF guard; no headless browser in V0.1.
- **Unreadable** (`blocked`, `fetch_failed`, `no_recipe`, `recipe_list`, `too_large`): nothing is saved; the app shows "Couldn't read that page" with the reason's message and **Type it in** opens with the link prefilled as provenance.
- **Duplicates** match on the provenance URL among live recipes (non-unique index on `household_id, url`): "Already in your library" with **Open it** or **Import again** (`force: true`). Manual entries are never duplicates.
- **HTTP bodies**: `201 imported` (with `needsReview`, `run`), `200 duplicate`, `400 bad_request`, `413 too_large` (6 MB), `422 unreadable` with `reason`, `426 update_shortcut`; every body carries a `message` the Shortcut shows verbatim.
- **Save first**: the recipe (source and provenance) and a durable `recipe_capture` row (trimmed gzip HTML, route, extractor version, report) are saved, then the engine runs inline with an 8 s budget. The stored capture is evidence and future extractor fixtures; it never rewrites the original text, and re-import from it is post-V0.1.
- **The Shortcut notification has no tap target**: `Saved "<title>". N lines to check in Finders Eaters.` The recipe tops the library under Recently added.
- **Not yet measured**: server-fetch reach from the Fly datacentre IP (measure against the seed sites during the build), and the Shortcut's on-device limits (CAP, the large-data prompt, the Get Contents of URL timeout), which [Run the iPhone Shortcut device checks](https://github.com/Ed-Barnes937/FindersEaters/issues/28) measures.

## 8. Food knowledge base

Authoritative: `docs/spec/food-knowledge-base.md`, coverage evidence in `docs/research/seed-coverage.md`.

- A **server-side build artifact** loaded into the `kb` schema by the release step when its version changes. Sources are pinned: the whole Open Food Facts English ingredients taxonomy, Intake24 item weights, USDA SR Legacy portions and densities, plus the reviewed **own layer** (YAML in the repo's `kb` package), curated through pull requests. Households never edit it.
- Every fact carries its source; precedence own > Intake24 > USDA > OFF.
- **Ingredients** have a stable surrogate UUID and a slug, and are retired (with `replaced_by`), never deleted.
- OFF edges split into "is a" (search, aisles, protein gating, always-have coverage, overlap) and "made from" (diet derivation only), with an ancestor closure over "is a".
- Seven tri-state dietary properties drive derived diet tags; the own layer owns allergens (beyond V0.1) and aisles.
- Own-layer properties added by later decisions: `whole_only` and `seasoning` (scaling), typical packs with a pack noun (shopping pack hint), the 9-aisle seed table, a short always-have suggestion list.
- **Resolution** is exact, parent or unresolved; there are no household-created ingredients. Each recipe records its KB version and is re-resolved lazily or in batch when it changes; corrections always win.
- The ODbL boundary is kept by layering; nothing is published in V0.1.

## 9. Engine

Authoritative: `docs/spec/engine-pipeline.md`, `docs/adr/0002-clef-on-workers-ai.md`, `docs/spec/tag-taxonomy.md`, `docs/research/golden-set.md`.

- A pure package, nine stages (normalise, segment, classify line kind, extract, resolve ingredient, resolve unit and unit system, canonicalise measure, recipe-level, validate and flag). Every decision goes **pin, then rule, then Clef over code-built candidates, then review flag**. No LLM. Code handles every number; Clef only picks among options code supplied. Every decided value records its source, and Clef decisions their confidence and top three alternatives.
- **Clef**: hosted Clef 27B on Cloudflare Workers AI (`@cf/cloudflare/clef`, base URL and model id from config). The v2 framing templates ship unchanged, one request per line plus one per recipe, at most 8 in parallel, kept-alive connections, about 5 s timeout, 2 retries on rate-limit and transient errors. The engine computes confidence itself from `probabilities` (Jev's normalised formula) and never reads the provider's field. Workers AI has no versioned ids, so instead of an exact model pin a **canary** (daily and before every release) fingerprints the answers and probabilities to the dev golden requests and opens an issue on a mismatch. Every Clef stage ships **`review-first`**; the [Run hosted Clef 27B on the held-out golden lines](https://github.com/Ed-Barnes937/FindersEaters/issues/19) result flips passing stages to `auto` (gates 0.8 in config, extraction 0.6 only if held-out accuracy at 0.6 is at least 0.95). This rerun does not block building.
- Below-gate resolutions and tags stay **unknown** with a one-tap suggestion. Unknown never matches a positive predicate.
- **Unit system** (UK, US, AU) is a recipe-level fact from a source-domain table (initial list in section 17.4), asked of Clef only for unknown domains with ambiguous units.
- **Tags**: 8 fixed dimensions (meal, cuisine, protein, carb, method, effort, equipment, diet). Diet is derived only. Time is a number, not a tag. Season is dropped. User tags are household facts used by search only.
- **Running**: inline with an 8 s budget; when Clef is unavailable or over budget the run is `partial` (rule-decided parts kept, "still being read" shown, no per-line flags) and is completed lazily on read and by the batch command. Review never blocks use.
- **Versions**: engine version = package version plus config hash (rules, lexicons, templates, model id and answer fingerprint, confidence formula, gates, stage modes, taxonomy version, plausibility limits, source-domain table) and triggers a full re-parse; a KB version change triggers partial re-resolution. The batch command prints a per-recipe diff and has a dry run.
- **Golden set**: dev (L01-L44, R01-R03) for tuning, held-out (L45-L194, R04-R20) for reporting; it is the engine's regression suite (section 19).

## 10. Review and correct

Authoritative: `docs/spec/recipe-review.md`, prototype `docs/prototypes/recipe-review/index.html`.

- Route `/recipes/:id/review`, reached after import when there is something to check, from the needs-review marker and banner, and from "Edit tags".
- One list in recipe order, two densities: **check lines** as cards (highlighted span, the question in words, up to 3 one-tap alternatives, free edit with ingredient autocomplete) and **glance lines** (review-first only) as compact original-over-reading rows. A tags block sits on top; tag suggestions are check severity.
- "Looks right - confirm N" confirms glance lines only, with a 4 s undo, and stores `confirm_bulk` corrections (pins, not golden candidates). Answering a line's last question confirms the rest of that line.
- A review-first 17-line recipe clears in about 7 taps, so review-first defaults stand.
- **Needs review** = at least one `check` flag whose target has no correction. Review needs a connection.

## 11. Recipes screen, search and recipe view

Authoritative: `docs/spec/search-and-library.md`, `docs/spec/query-model.md`.

- **One Recipes screen** (`/recipes?q=&sort=`): text box with bare-word typeahead to predicates, active chips, a Filters sheet and sort. It doubles as the planner's anchor picker (pick mode). Back restores query, sort and scroll.
- **Query model**: a versioned and/or/not tree of predicates over a registered field set (8 dimensions, `tag:`, `ing:` with "is a" hierarchy, `time:<30`, `is:favourite`, `cooked:`, title words). Filters only, never sorts. Unknown never matches; negation includes it; `dim:unknown` asks for it. One parameterised, household-scoped SQL query. Text form in the URL; the server is authoritative and returns the parsed tree or errors with positions.
- **Filter sheet**: rows in cook's decision order (meal, time, protein, carb, cuisine, diet, ingredients, then the rest), only values the library has (plus any already used), no unknown chips, tri-state chips, an always-visible diet note.
- **Sort**: Recently added (default), A to Z, Quickest, Longest since cooked; sort lives beside `q`, never in it.
- **States**: empty library offers import; no matches offers removing chips; a parse error underlines the span, offers "did you mean" and shows no results.
- **List row**: text only (no images): title, facts line (time, effort, cuisine), provenance and last cooked, favourite toggle, needs-review marker.
- **Recipe view** (`/recipes/:id`): provenance, favourite, facts, fixed tags (each a search link), the dietary note under any diet tag, user tags (inline add and remove), servings stepper with factor badge, ingredients (scaled, original text at x1), method, Add to plan. Opened from a plan item (`?item=`), the stepper edits that item ("For this plan").
- **Delete** asks first, says what else is affected ("It stays in 2 past plans"), removes upcoming plan items, and is a soft delete.

## 12. Scaling

Authoritative: `docs/spec/scaling.md`.

- One pure, shared `scale(recipeOutput, target, kb)`, used by the recipe view and the shopping list (per-line **use amount**), so they never disagree.
- Factor = target / yield N; null or unknown yield = x1; a ranged yield uses its lower bound. No separate multiplier: the stepper shows a factor badge. Makes-recipes step in half or whole batches. Unknown servings show "Set servings" (a recipe correction) and "(as written)" on the list.
- At x1 the original text shows; otherwise only the quantity span is replaced, units kept, nearest kitchen rounding (display units in section 17.1).
- Two-measure arithmetic scales termwise for display; shopping evaluates canonical, then same-dimension unit facts, then plus = both terms and minus = the first.
- **Scaling notes** are view-only (never stored, never flags): `whole_only` rounding (also feeds shopping), part pack, seasoning above x1.5, and one recipe-level "times and tin sizes as written".

## 13. Planning and suggestions

Authoritative: `docs/spec/planner-screen.md`, `docs/spec/suggestion-scoring.md`, `docs/spec/plan-lifecycle.md`, prototype `docs/prototypes/plan-builder/index.html`.

**Plan screen**

- One screen for the current plan: plan items on top, suggestions inline below as dashed cards, plus Add all. Nothing is in the plan until added.
- Anchors come from the Recipes screen in pick mode or Add to plan on a recipe view.
- The **suggest bar** asks for N more of one meal type, with the Recipes filter sheet for an optional extra query. One group per request.
- A card: title, time and effort, at most two positive reasons, "Why this?" (signed bars, no numbers), Add, Another (skip in place), Swap (next five, or search, straight into the plan).
- Reasons are recomputed against the current set on every change; a slot changes only by its own skip or swap.
- Days and servings live on plan items only (day picker; undated is the default). A ranged plan shows its empty days.
- First run, pool running out ("Only 3 dinners left to suggest") and empty pool states as in the planner spec and design brief.

**Scoring** (`suggest(request, context)`, pure)

- Hard layer: the request's group query through the query executor, not already in the plan, not skipped (later: rule maxima).
- Soft score = the sum of five fixed-weight terms: overlap +1.0 (shared ingredients via "is a", weighted by smoothed library IDF, always-haves excluded, capped), favourite +0.6, effort +0.3, recently cooked -0.5 (one 28-day default gap, nearest cooking either side), variety -1.0 (protein, carb, cuisine, method; 1 - 0.5^count).
- Every term is a structured reason whose contributions sum to the score, with a zero-contribution "fresh" fallback.
- Greedy, set-aware, deterministic selection; skip re-picks one slot. Suggestions are not persisted. Presets are out.

**Lifecycle**

- A plan's shopping list covers every item in it, whatever the date. Ticks reset only when the household starts a new plan by hand.
- A **finished plan** (range ended, or no range and nothing dated today or later) shows a banner nudging New plan.
- **New plan** (date chips This week, Next 7 days, Pick dates, No dates) moves the items not cooked yet (undated or dated today or later) with "Bring over N recipes not cooked yet", ticked by default, and carries their ticks, which show At home as "Bought for last plan".
- Past plans open from the header menu with a "Past plan" banner, stay editable (they are cooking history), offer no suggestions, and show a read-only list. Deleting the current plan makes the previous one current.

## 14. Shopping list

Authoritative: `docs/spec/shopping-list.md`, `docs/spec/plan-lifecycle.md`, prototype `docs/prototypes/shopping-list/index.html`.

- **Computed on every read** by `buildList` from the plan's items and each recipe's effective output, scaled per plan item through `scale`. No stored list rows; the only stored state is `shopping_tick`.
- **Item keys**: one item per ingredient id; no merging up the hierarchy; unresolved lines merge only on identical normalised text (`unresolved:<text>`).
- **Amounts**: a canonical group plus per-unit as-written groups plus "some"; display-only round-up; a pack hint; ranges use max; unquantified-only items show no amount.
- **Always-have coverage**: same ingredient, its "is a" descendants and ancestors, never siblings. Covered items start ticked in At home with no snapshot.
- **Serve-with items**: carbs derived from the applied carb tag, shown unquantified.
- **Optional lines** contribute like any line; their source entry says "(optional)", and an item whose every contribution is optional shows an "optional" badge.
- **Aisles**: a fixed 9-aisle walk order inherited down "is a" from a seed table; unresolved lines go under "other".
- **Ticks**: per (plan, item key) with device time, a snapshot of the amounts seen, and origin `tap` or `carried`. Later device tick wins. A ticked item whose need now exceeds its snapshot shows unticked with "was N".
- **In the shop**: one whole-row tap per ingredient with 4 s undo; ticked rows collapse to In the basket; At home section; "N of M to get"; each item shows its source lines ("for Chilli, Dal").
- **Offline**: the phone caches the last list per plan; taps queue as tick writes coalesced per key and replay on reconnect. The tick mutation input is a frozen contract (section 15).
- Shared shopping freshness: section 17.5.

## 15. Operations

Authoritative: `docs/spec/operations.md`.

- **Environments**: `finders-eaters` (main) and `finders-eaters-staging` (develop, its own Neon branch, holding real household data until a second household exists).
- **Release step** (Fly `release_command`): migrate under an advisory lock, then load the KB and rebuild projections when their versions change, and report stale recipes.
- **Migrations**: expand-only per release, contract in a later release, enforced by a CI lint on destructive SQL. Derived data is never migrated (version bumps instead). Fix forward; rollback = previous image.
- **Client compatibility**: tRPC inputs change additively only; the tick mutation input is frozen (it only gains optional fields; a shape change is a new procedure kept for at least 30 days); `/api/v1` never breaks; a new service worker activates on next launch, never mid-session.
- **Backups**: Neon's 7-day restore window plus a nightly GitHub Actions `pg_dump` to Tigris (35 daily, 400-day monthly), each restored into a pg17 container and verified. Staging is refreshed from the latest backup before every release.
- **Batch re-parse**: `fly machine run` one-off, dry run then apply, staging first.
- **Human go-live steps** (one wizard ticket): Neon project, branches and restore window, `backup` role, Tigris bucket and lifecycle rules, both Fly apps, tokens and secrets, Cloudflare DNS records, the Cloudflare account ID and a Workers AI API token, creating the household and users with the CLI.

## 17. Decisions closed in this spec

These were open in the map's "Not yet specified" and would otherwise block slicing. All are **agent-decided and reversible** (two-way doors); none changes the durable data model beyond what is stated.

### 17.1 Display of units

- The household `units` setting has one value in V0.1, `uk_metric`, with no UI.
- **Recipe view, x1**: the original text, always.
- **Recipe view, scaled**: only the quantity span changes, in the line's own unit. Metric mass and volume step up at 1000 (g to kg, ml to l) with at most one decimal, trailing ".0" dropped ("1.2 kg", "1 l"). Spoons stay spoons; counts stay counts with the line's own noun ("2 onions"). Rounding to kitchen precision follows `scaling.md`.
- **Metric hint for non-metric lines**: a line written in cups, oz, lb, fl oz or pints, or with an Australian tablespoon, shows a muted metric equivalent after the amount ("1 cup flour (125 g)", "1 tbsp (20 ml)") when the line has a canonical amount; no hint when there is no conversion path. The hint never replaces the original or scaled text. UK spoons and metric lines get no hint.
- **Shopping list**: canonical groups display in metric (g/kg, ml/l with the same step-up) or as natural counts with the ingredient's singular or plural name ("2 onions", "1 lemon"); as-written groups keep their unit ("2 tins"). Pack hints and "some" per `shopping-list.md` section 4.3.
- A later household-units choice (US cups, imperial) applies on top of the scaled amount and only to hints and the shopping list; it needs a second enum value, not a migration.

### 17.2 Test and evaluation strategy

Quality bar: no flaky tests tolerated (CI retries are 0; a flaky test is a bug), lint and type errors fail CI, and bug fixes start with a reproducing test at the highest level that shows the bug.

| Layer | What | How |
|---|---|---|
| Pure modules | `engine` rules, `query`, `scale`, `suggest`, `buildList`, `kb` build and validation | Vitest, table-driven and property tests (query print/parse round trip; `scale` at x1 returns the original text; `buildList` is order-independent over plan items). |
| Engine regression | The golden set | CI runs dev and held-out against **recorded Clef answers** (cassettes keyed by framing version, model id, answer fingerprint and request hash) on every PR touching the engine or KB; a missing cassette fails. A live held-out run is a manual workflow required for any framing, model, fingerprint or gate change, and refreshes the cassettes. The **canary** runs the dev requests live daily and before every release and opens an issue when the fingerprint changes. Pass rule per `docs/research/golden-set.md`. |
| Planner evaluation | `suggest` | Fixture scenarios from `suggestion-scoring.md` section 8 and the plan-lifecycle scenarios (scenario 2, the lentils carry-over) as named tests; synthetic libraries of 30 and 60 recipes built from golden-set recipes with property checks (deterministic output; no two picks in one request share protein, carb and cuisine when an alternative exists; the overlap cap and distinctiveness curve behave as specified). Before weights are frozen for go-live, an owner review of suggestions on the real library snapshot (staging) via a `suggest-report` CLI command that prints picks and reasons. |
| Database | Migrations, data access, query compiler | One `pgvector/pgvector:pg17` container per test run (CI service, Docker Compose locally); migrations applied once; each test in a rolled-back transaction. Includes the household-scope test (every household-owned table is filtered), the query SQL conformance suite (unknown, negation, hierarchy, NULL cases), compare-and-set races, and the destructive-SQL lint. |
| API | tRPC routers, import route, auth | Server-side callers against the test database with a fake Clef client (cassette-backed); auth tests per `auth.md` section 8 (token hashing, revoke, cookie ignored on the import route, rate limits, cross-household access denied). |
| End to end | The core loop in a browser | Playwright against the production build (server plus SPA) on a 390 px mobile viewport, with a seeded database, the KB loaded, a cassette-backed Clef stub server and a local static site serving fixture recipe pages. Flows: sign in; paste-link import then review and confirm; manual entry; search with filters and a parse error; build a plan with suggestions, Another and Swap; shopping list ticking, offline ticking with replay (Playwright offline context), "was N" after a plan grows; New plan with carry-over. Runs on every PR. |
| Operations | Backups and release | The nightly restore-and-verify job and the staging refresh (`operations.md`) are the restore tests; a CI job runs the release step against a fresh container to prove migrations and the KB load apply from empty. |

### 17.4 Source-domain to unit-system table (initial list)

Engine configuration (part of the engine version). Matching is on the registrable domain; country TLDs decide first.

- **UK**: any `.co.uk` or `.uk`; `bbcgoodfood.com`, `bbc.co.uk`, `jamieoliver.com`, `nigella.com`, `deliciousmagazine.co.uk`, `olivemagazine.com`, `theguardian.com`, `waitrose.com`, `sainsburysmagazine.co.uk`, `donalskehan.com`.
- **AU**: any `.com.au` or `.au` (including `taste.com.au`, `delicious.com.au`); `recipetineats.com`.
- **US**: `seriouseats.com`, `allrecipes.com`, `nytimes.com` (NYT Cooking), `bonappetit.com`, `epicurious.com`, `foodnetwork.com`, `simplyrecipes.com`, `budgetbytes.com`, `kingarthurbaking.com`, `smittenkitchen.com`, `food52.com`.
- **Manual entry** (no domain) and unknown domains follow engine stage 6 unchanged: only when the recipe uses an ambiguous unit is Clef asked to choose the system, and below the gate UK (the household default) is applied and flagged `unit_system_assumed`.

The list grows by pull request as imports show `unit_system_assumed` flags.

### 17.5 Shared shopping freshness

No push in V0.1. While the Shop screen is open and online, the client refetches the list on focus, on reconnect and every 20 s, so two shoppers converge within seconds without a socket. Ticks still merge by later device time.

### 17.6 App-wide Clef spend guard

Engine configuration carries a daily app-wide Clef request cap (default 2,000 requests, about 130 average recipes, about $1.60 at Clef's $0.24 per million input tokens; Workers AI's free 10,000 neurons a day cover roughly the first 140 requests). The canary's requests count against it. Requests are counted per UTC day in an ops table. Over the cap, runs end `partial` through the existing path and complete lazily after the reset; the batch re-parse command refuses to start an apply that would exceed the remaining budget unless given an explicit override. A structured log line and the `/health` detail report the day's usage.

### 17.7 Smaller items confirmed

- **Plausibility thresholds** (5 kg, 5 l per line, zero) stay first guesses in engine configuration, so changing them is an engine version bump with a re-parse diff.
- **Optional ingredient lines** count for diet derivation and for the shopping list (`shopping-list.md` section 6); "vegetarian if you skip X" is later.
- **Household timezone** is set by the CLI (default `Europe/London`).
