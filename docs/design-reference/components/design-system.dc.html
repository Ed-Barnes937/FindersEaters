Design system and the three hardest components

Warm paper and ink, one rosemary-green accent, butter yellow reserved for the uncertain span. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next carries every piece of UI text. Newsreader is used only for screen titles, so the kitchen feels a little warmer without costing legibility. Tokens are in tokens.css.

1 · Shopping row

The whole row is the target, 64 px minimum. The tick circle sits on the left where a right thumb lands when the phone is held in the left hand on a trolley handle, and amounts align right in tabular figures so they scan as a column. Badges are words, never colour alone.

3 · Review: check card, glance line, clear line

Three densities with three weights. A check card is a raised card with a question as its heading and the answers as large buttons. A glance line is a flat row with a tick to confirm. A clear line is muted text only. The original always sits on top, in a well, with the uncertain words highlighted and underlined.

Notes for round 1

  • Suggestions vs plan items: no fill and a dashed edge, plus the "Suggested" heading. Works in greyscale.
  • Refreshed reasons cross-fade text only; a replaced recipe (Another) gets a 2 s fill and "New pick". Reduced motion: no fade, the "New pick" label stays until the next change.
  • Check card answers are stacked full-width buttons, not chips, because "5 cm piece" and "Buy it, or optional garnish" vary a lot in length and need 48 px targets.
  • Copy suggestion: "Looks right - confirm 1" reads better as "Confirm 1 that looks right" or simply "Confirm 1". The hyphen looks like a typo on a button.
